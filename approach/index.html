How I approach a problem

Five projects. The result first; open a card for why it mattered, what we did and the bump along the way.

01 · Reshape Biotech · Product launch

Launching the robot

Took Reshape’s first quality-control product into a new segment.

Result
  • 100+ attendees from biotech companies worldwide
  • The launch influenced ~$0.5M in pipeline
+
Why
Reshape’s imaging technology could serve quality control (QC), but QC buyers worry about different things than our existing customers. The product team built a robot for QC labs, the High-throughput Imaging Device, and I owned the launch end to end.
How
Repositioned the message around what QC cares about: time savings, data accuracy and a clear trail of every test when an auditor asks. Trained sales together with a co-founder, rewrote the website, made the launch videos with a videographer and our designer, ran a 45-minute online launch event and sent four email campaigns to four different audiences.
The bump
Software was built after the hardware, but the videos and sales training needed a working product. Together with our designer I built clickable mockups showing how software and robot would work together, so the launch date didn’t have to wait.

Positioning | Sales training | Video | Launch event | Email

02 · Reshape Biotech · User onboarding

Onboarding every new user, not just the admins

A five-email, 30-day programme that reaches people while they are still in the product.

Result
  • ~80% open rate across all five emails
  • ~35% click-through on the first email
  • 200–250 new users onboarded
+
Why
Administrators told me that the colleagues they invited landed in the product without knowing how to use it. Admins got a hands-on onboarding; everyone else got an invitation and silence.
How
Five emails over 30 days, following the work a new user actually does, each linking to a help article with a video guide. I wrote the copy, built the flow in Intercom, scripted and edited the videos and wrote the articles, with a data engineer from the marketing team on the data side and a customer success manager in front of the camera.
The bump
The first email arrived far too late because product data synced once a day. We restructured the sync to run every 30 minutes, so the first email lands within half an hour of accepting the invitation.

Lifecycle | Intercom | Video guides | Help centre

03 · Pleo · UK relaunch

Letting customers name the product

Relaunched Pleo Bills as Pleo Invoices in the UK, and took it to market through bookkeeping partners.

Result
  • 200 bookkeeping partners on board in about six months
  • #2 Product of the Day on Product Hunt
+
Why
Pleo wanted to cover all company spend, not just card expenses, and invoices are a big share of it. A first UK attempt had run for about a year without traction.
How
In about 20 customer conversations, nine times out of ten the people who said “bills” added “we mean invoices”. So we renamed it, and the rename became the hook. I owned positioning, messaging and the go-to-market through partners: an enablement portal, a campaign video, website copy, targeted emails and a Product Hunt launch, with partner marketing, the brand team and two product teams.
The bump
Everyone involved had competing priorities. Instead of asking for “more time”, I negotiated something concrete: two weeks of a motion designer and a dedicated budget for the launch.

Naming | Positioning | Partner enablement | Video

04 · Pleo · PR around funding rounds

Making funding news worth a second story

Took the CEO’s theme of attracting international talent to Denmark into the national debate.

Result
  • National TV and front pages of Børsen and Finans
  • Roughly 3–4× the job applications after each round (est.)
+
Why
Funding rounds put Pleo in the spotlight and brought in applicants, but “startup raises money” gets old fast for journalists.
How
Owned all Danish media contact across Series A, B, C and a C extension. Wrote the press releases, gave each outlet its own angle, prepared the CEO with labour-market figures and kept pitching his talent theme through the Series B and the Danish election campaign.
The bump
I learned the hard way not to pitch the same story to every outlet. From then on each got its own angle or an exclusive, which also helped on busy news days.

PR | Press releases | Media relations | Employer brand

05 · Pleo · Targeted campaign

A campaign for ~300 supported housing facilities

A small, well-defined audience, reached through their own national organisation.

Result
  • ~30–40 booked meetings in two months
+
Why
Customer conversations kept showing the same thing: staff paid for residents’ shopping out of a shared cash box, and bookkeepers couldn’t see where the money went.
How
Mapped the ~300 facilities in Denmark into one list. Reached them through their national organisation’s newsletter and members’ magazine, display ads on sites care staff read, print ads with a QR code and paid campaigns run by our paid team, all pointing to one landing page with a first-year discount.
The bump
The print and display partners couldn’t measure much. Tracked links and QR codes, matched against booked meetings, showed which channel actually worked.

Campaign | Segmentation | Print + display | HubSpot
