How I approach a problem

Five case studies from Reshape Biotech and Pleo. The result comes first; open a case for why it mattered, what I did, the decision that shaped it and what went wrong along the way.

Reshape Biotech · Product launch

Launching new hardware

I led product marketing for Reshape’s launch into quality-control labs.

Result
  • 100+ attendees from biotech companies worldwide at the launch event
  • In the weeks after the launch, companies that attended accounted for ~$0.5M in new pipeline
+
My role
Launch positioning, communications and sales preparation
With
The hardware and software teams, a co-founder, a freelance videographer and our designer
Timeline
About three months from first preparation to the launch campaign
Why
Reshape’s imaging technology could serve quality control (QC), but QC buyers worry about different things than our existing customers. The product team built a robot for QC labs, the High-throughput Imaging Device, and I led launch positioning, communications and sales preparation.
How
I moved the message from data harmonisation to what QC cares about: time saved, accurate data and a clear trail of every test when an auditor asks. I trained sales together with a co-founder, rewrote the website and made the launch videos with a videographer and our designer. We ran a 45-minute online launch event and four email campaigns.
The call
I split the follow-up by how warm each audience was. Cold leads got a longer sequence that built trust, with our ISO certifications and examples of how the hardware is used. Warmer leads got a short sequence that went straight to the launch video and the website.
The bump
Software was built after the hardware, but the videos and sales training needed a working product. Together with our designer I built clickable mockups showing how software and robot would work together, so the launch date didn’t have to wait.

Reshape Biotech · User onboarding

Onboarding every new user, not just the admins

I built a five-email programme that starts within 30 minutes of an accepted invitation and runs for 30 days.

Result
  • 200–250 new users received the emails
  • ~80% average open rate across the five emails
  • ~35% clicked through from the first email
+
My role
Built and ran the programme: copy, flow, videos and help articles
With
A marketing data engineer, a customer success manager and the product team
Timeline
About a week to build; 30 days for each new user
Why
Administrators told me that the colleagues they invited landed in the product without knowing how to use it. Admins got a hands-on onboarding from customer success; everyone else got an invitation and silence.
How
Each email follows the work a new user actually does: a platform overview, starting a job, analysing the data, validating results and trying other experiments. Each links to a help article with a video guide. I wrote the copy, built the flow in Intercom, scripted and edited the videos and wrote the articles. A customer success manager presented the videos.
The call
I left out the administrators. They already had a personal onboarding with customer success, and a second, automated one would only have got in the way.
The bump
The first email arrived far too late, because product data synced only once a day. I worked with a marketing data engineer to run the sync every 30 minutes, so the first email now arrives within half an hour.

Pleo · Product relaunch

Letting customers name the product

I led product marketing for relaunching Pleo Bills as Pleo Invoices in the UK, through bookkeeping partners.

Result
  • 200 bookkeeping partners signed up and onboarded at least one client within about six months
  • #2 Product of the Day on Product Hunt
+
My role
Positioning, messaging and the go-to-market through partners
With
Partner marketing, the brand team, two product teams and the head of bookkeeping partnerships
Timeline
Partners came on board over about six months
Why
Pleo wanted to cover all company spend, not just card expenses, and invoices are a big share of it. A first UK attempt had run for about a year without traction.
How
In around 20 customer conversations, people kept clarifying that by “bills” they meant invoices. So we renamed the product, and the rename became the hook. I built an enablement portal for partners, directed a campaign video made by a motion designer, wrote the website copy and ran targeted emails and a Product Hunt launch.
The call
We took it to market through bookkeeping partners rather than straight to companies. Partners wanted to gather their clients’ finances in fewer tools, the main UK competitor had become more expensive, and Pleo already had traction with its card.
The bump
Everyone involved had competing priorities. Instead of asking for “more time”, I secured two weeks of a motion designer’s time and a dedicated launch budget.
Pleo · Press relations

Making funding news worth a second story

I pitched Danish media stories about the CEO’s case for attracting international talent to Denmark.

Result
  • National TV and front pages of Børsen and Finans
  • Roughly 3–4× the job applications compared with before the coverage (my estimate)
+
My role
All Danish media contact around the funding rounds
With
The CEO and the head of brand
Timeline
Series A, B, C and a C extension
Why
Funding rounds put Pleo in the spotlight and brought in applicants, but “start-up raises money” gets old fast for journalists.
How
I wrote the press releases, gave each outlet its own angle and prepared the CEO with figures on the Danish labour market. The talent theme was the CEO’s; I kept pitching it through the Series B and the Danish election campaign.
The call
I gave the funding news a political angle. The size of a round interests the business press once; Denmark’s shortage of tech talent gave TV and the papers a reason to come back.
The bump
I learned the hard way not to pitch the same story to every outlet. From then on, each got its own angle or an exclusive, which also helped on busy news days.
Pleo · Targeted campaign

A campaign for ~300 supported housing facilities

A small, well-defined audience, reached through their own national organisation.

Result
  • ~30–40 sales meetings booked with supported housing facilities during the two-month campaign
+
My role
Overall responsibility for the campaign
With
Our designer, the paid media team and sales
Timeline
Two months
Why
Customer conversations kept showing the same thing: staff paid for residents’ shopping from a shared cash box, and bookkeepers struggled to track the spending.
How
I mapped the ~300 facilities in Denmark into one list. We reached them with display ads on sites care staff read, print ads with a QR code and paid campaigns, all pointing to one landing page with a first-year discount.
The call
Instead of buying broad reach, I went to the sector’s own national organisation and placed the offer in its newsletter and members’ magazine.
The bump
The print and display partners couldn’t measure much. I matched tracked links and QR codes against booked meetings to compare how each channel performed.
